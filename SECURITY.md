SECURITY POLICY

SEC. 1. SCOPE.

    (a) IN GENERAL.—This policy applies to each owned system and, under section 10, to each Prinzian system.

    (b) PULL REQUESTS.—The V31null Hub and standartas are open source. A reporter may submit a problem in the V31null Hub or in standartas as a pull request on https://github.com/v31null/v31nhub or https://github.com/v31null/standartas, respectively.

SEC. 2. DEFINITIONS.

    In this policy:

        (1) ACCESS.—The term “access” means any action on data, including—
            (A) viewing, reading, listening to, or watching the data;
            (B) copying, downloading, recording, capturing on screen, or storing the data;
            (C) changing, adding to, reordering, or moving the data;
            (D) deleting the data or making the data unreadable; and
            (E) sending the data to any person or system.

        (2) ACCOUNT OF THE REPORTER.—The term “account of the reporter” means anything that the reporter created or installed and controls, including—
            (A) an account on Prono;
            (B) an operating system;
            (C) a local virtual machine;
            (D) a container or emulator;
            (E) a physical device;
            (F) a local install of a programme;
            (G) a local copy of a file or repository; and
            (H) an account on the V31N Portal or on any owned system.
            
        (3) ANOTHER SYSTEM.—The term “another system” means any system other than the system in which a problem is found, including another owned system and a third party.

        (4) ANOTHER USER.—The term “another user” means a natural person, and not an automated system or machine account, other than the reporter who uses, operates, or administers an owned system, including—
            (A) another account holder;
            (B) a Help Desk operator or registration officer; and
            (C) an administrator of v31null.

        (5) BACKDOOR.—The term “backdoor” means a means of access that the reporter places in an owned system and that remains after the test ends, including—
            (A) an account, session, token, or key;
            (B) a file or program; and
            (C) a changed setting or permission.

        (5A) CRAFTED .V31NP FILE.—The term “crafted .v31np file” means a .v31np file that the reporter builds or alters to make a reader of .v31np files fail.

        (6) DATA.—The term “data” means any information that relates to a user, whether stored or in transit, including—
            (A) a message, reply, reaction, like, or pin;
            (B) an attachment, avatar, ambiance image, group image, or emoji;
            (C) a telegram;
            (D) a name, PIN, email address, password, or setting;
            (E) a session, token, cookie, or key;
            (F) a friendship, friend request, or block;
            (G) a membership in a group, server, channel, or theatre;
            (H) a read state, presence, status, or activity;
            (I) a log entry or record about the user;
            (J) an FRNI and any personal details submitted to compute or verify an FRNI;
            (K) a Help Desk ticket, message, attachment, identity document, or third-party detail appearing on such a document;
            (L) an eID application, approval record, eID certificate, or revocation record; and
            (M) an OIDC link, authorization code, access token, recovery booking, recovery event, or sealed data copy.

        (7) DELETE.—The term “delete” means to remove each copy that the reporter holds, including a screenshot, recording, note, and backup.

        (8) DELIVER.—The term “deliver” means to make available to another user, including through a message, attachment, avatar, ambiance image, group image, emoji, or link.

        (8A) DEVELOPER.—The term “developer” means a person who develops a Prinzian system or offers a Prinzian system for use.

        (9) FIX.—A problem is fixed when the conditions in section 4(b)(6)(D) are met.

        (9A) FRNI.—The terms “FRNI” and “P.I. №” have the same meaning and denote a fifteen-digit Private Personal Identification Number assigned by the V31N Portal, including its internal lookup, hash, and sequence values and the registration or recovery source details used to compute it.

        (10) HARMFUL FILE.—The term “harmful file” means a file made to damage, take control of, or take data from a device or program that opens or processes the file, including a crafted .v31np file.

        (10A) INTENDED BEHAVIOUR.—The term “intended behaviour” means a behaviour of an owned system that v31null sets out as intended, including in a standard, in documentation, or in code.

        (10B) INTERVENE.—The term “intervene” means to take one or more of the following measures, in the following order—
            (A) a binding finding, including on whether a system falls under this policy;
            (B) an order to show cause;
            (C) an order to comply;
            (D) filling a gap in the security declaration policy of an owner with the text of this policy;
            (E) sending a Practicer General;
            (F) where the Practicer General is not accepted, a network ban; and
            (G) after a network ban, a suspension of eIDs, connection tokens, API keys, or connection permits.

        (10C) LEGAL MINIMUM.—The term “legal minimum” means the negative powers only.

        (10D) MEF.—The term “MEF” means the Mbrlí úír Entípatùíțù fùțbíz (Committee for Public Security).

        (10E) MEF SYSTEM.—The term “MEF system” means a Prinzian system owned by MEF or by an agency under MEF, including each owned system.

        (10F) NEGATIVE POWERS.—The term “negative powers” means the prohibitions and duties that this policy imposes, which separate into—
            (A) the negative powers of the tester, being the duties of a reporter to ensure that the reporter tests safely, as set out in section 4(b) other than paragraph (6), section 4(c), section 4(d), section 4(h), and section 6(b); and
            (B) the negative powers of the developer, being the duty of a developer to ensure safe programmes.

        (10G) NON-INTERESTING POWERS.—The term “non-interesting powers” means the form and procedure of this policy for MEF systems, as set out in section 3, section 4(b)(6), section 4(e), section 5, section 8, and section 9.

        (11) OWNED SYSTEM.—The term “owned system” means—
            (A) each host and service that v31null operates;
            (B) each Prono client and the V31null Hub;
            (C) each public repository of v31null;
            (D) each programme, installer, build, and release that v31null publishes, now or in the future;
            (E) the V31N Portal; and
            (F) each trust service operated by v31null.

        (12) PERMANENT ACCESS.—The term “permanent access” means any ability to access an owned system that remains after the test ends.

        (12A) POSITIVE POWERS.—The term “positive powers” means the authorizations and protections that this policy grants, as set out in section 4(a), section 4(f), section 4(g), section 6(a), and section 7.

        (12B) PRACTICER GENERAL.—The term “Practicer General” means the compliance officer of MEF, appointed by MEF to oversee standards, policy, law, by-laws, and similar legal compliance.

        (12C) PRINZIAN SYSTEM.—The term “Prinzian system” means any system, standard, programme, service, & hoc genus omne developed or offered for use in Prinz, including any that uses, relies on, or connects to any Prinzian standard, system, network, service, or identity, & hoc genus omne. The use of any one of them suffices.

        (13) PROBLEM.—
            (A) IN GENERAL.—The term “problem” means a flaw in an owned system through which a person can act beyond what the owned system permits that person.
            (B) EXCLUSION.—The term “problem” does not include social engineering, meaning the deception of a user into giving a person access, data, or an action. Social engineering is a matter of the user and not of an owned system.
            (C) INTENDED BEHAVIOUR.—The term “problem” does not include an intended behaviour.
            (D) CONFORMANCE.—The term “problem” does not include the conformance of a programme or file with a standard of v31null. v31null is not a conformance agency. Developers see to conformance on their own or through an agency that they establish.
            (E) RESOURCE OR QUOTA EXHAUSTION.—The term “problem” does not include the exhaustion, saturation, or triggering of any finite quota, capacity, slot, storage, bandwidth, or rate limit by repeatedly or excessively invoking a normal function of an owned system or of a third party, nor any failure, slowdown, or unavailability resulting from that exhaustion.
            (F) NON-PUBLIC TEST ENVIRONMENTS AND SPECIAL ACCESS.—The term “problem” does not include a condition that exists solely within, or can be reached only by means of, a non-public test sandbox or special access granted personally to the reporter, unless the affected component, isolation boundary, or flaw is reachable by the public or shared with a public or production owned system.


        (14) PRONO.—The term “Prono” means the Services for þe General populace Maß Tele-kommunikation services namen as prono.

            (A) a web browser;
            (B) the pronal desktop application for Windows and for Linux; and
            (C) the pronal application for Android.

        (15A) PROTECTED SERVICE.—The term “protected service” means any owned system or component that may not be taken down, disrupted, or degraded even once, including—
            (A) each trust service;

        (16) PUBLISH.—
            (A) IN GENERAL.—The term “publish” means to make information available to any person other than v31null.
            (B) REPORTS TO AUTHORITIES.—The term “publish” does not include a report of a problem to an authority competent for the matter in the country of residence or of nationality of the reporter, if the reporter has first sent the report to v31null under section 3.         (19) TAKE THE SERVICE DOWN.—The term “take the service down” means to make an owned system unavailable to users or to slow the owned system so that users cannot use it.

        (20) REPEALED.

        (21) TEST.—The term “test” means any action taken to find, confirm, or demonstrate a problem.

        (22) THIRD PARTY.—The term “third party” means a provider on which an owned system relies, including—

SEC. 4. CONDUCT.

    (a) PERMITTED ACTIONS.—A reporter may—
        (1) test an owned system, including through a third party that carries traffic to the owned system;
        (2) unpack, decompile, or otherwise take apart a Prono client, the V31null Hub, or any programme, installer, build, or release that v31null publishes;
        (3) create accounts;
        (4) run automated scanners;
        (5) take the service down once to prove a problem, except a protected service under section 2(15A);
        (6) upload a harmful file to an account of the reporter;
        (7) direct a message, request, or notification to an account of the reporter; and
        (8) access the data of an account of the reporter.

    (b) PROHIBITED ACTIONS.—A reporter may not—
        (1) test or attack a third party;
        (2) access the data of another user;
        (3) leave a backdoor, keep permanent access, or use a problem to enter another system;
        (4) deliver a harmful file to another user;
        (5) direct a message, request, or notification to another user as part of a test;
        (6) publish a problem before v31null fixes the problem, subject to the following:
            (A) ACKNOWLEDGMENT AND FORM.—
                (i) IN GENERAL.—A report shall be acknowledged by v31null in the official use block of the form under section 5, and an approximate duration for handling or remediation may be noted in the margins or fields of that block.
                (ii) ACTUAL ACKNOWLEDGMENT.—Once v31null actually acknowledges a report, the report is deemed acknowledged ipso facto and ab initio, unless and until v31null states otherwise.
            (B) RESPONSE PERIOD.—The expected response period for v31null to acknowledge a report is—
                (i) 14 days for a report submitted on Prono under section 3(a); and
                (ii) 30 days for a report submitted over any other platform, including GitHub under section 3(b).
            (C) EFFECT OF NON-RESPONSE OR DELAY.—If v31null does not respond within the applicable period under subparagraph (B), or does not resolve the problem within any approximate duration noted under subparagraph (A)(i), the prohibition on publication under this paragraph remains in full force. Silence or delay does not lift the prohibition.
            (D) WHAT CONSTITUTES A FIX.—For purposes of this policy and section 2(9), a problem counts as fixed only when—
                (i) v31null has deployed a patch, released an update, merged a remediation, or otherwise resolved or closed the flaw; and
                (ii) v31null expressly notifies the reporter, directly or by updating the official use block of the form under section 5, that the problem is fixed;
            (E) INCOMPLETE FIXES, BYPASSES, AND REGRESSIONS.—If a reporter, while verifying a fix or conducting permitted testing, discovers that a fix is incomplete, can be bypassed, or has introduced a new problem, the reporter shall report that bypass or new problem under section 3 and section 5, and the prohibition on publication under this paragraph applies until that bypass or new problem is itself fixed under subparagraph (D);
        (7) make a threat in connection with a report;
        (8) test an intended behaviour, including with a payload;
        (9) submit a report on conformance;
        (10) attempt to decode, reverse, brute-force, or derive source details from any FRNI, including an FRNI of an account of the reporter, or probe any endpoint to enumerate FRNIs or sequence numbers;
        (11) as part of a test, mint, forge, alter, or fraudulently obtain a v31null eID, electronic seal, certificate revocation list, certification authority revocation list, OCSP response, or trusted list entry, abuse the signing service, or trigger a false revocation, except that a person who encounters such a flaw during ordinary use without conducting a prohibited test may report it;
        (12) search for, access, extract, decrypt, copy, or retain any sensitive material;
        (13) cause resource or quota exhaustion described in section 2(13)(E), or upload a harmful file to a Help Desk ticket;
        (14) act recklessly as to whether a test impairs, disrupts, or degrades a protected service, or impairs, disrupts, or degrades any other owned system beyond the single demonstration permitted under subsection (a)(5), regardless of whether impairment was intended; or
        (15) create any exploit tool, script, or payload generator targeting an owned system with criminal intent, or distribute, publish, sell, or supply to any person other than v31null, whether before or after the underlying problem is fixed, any exploit tool, script, payload generator, or weaponized proof of concept targeting an owned system where it is likely to be used for unauthorized access or harm, except that submitting a proof of concept privately to v31null in a report under section 5 or submitting a remediation pull request under section 1(b) is permitted.

    (c) DATA OF OTHER USERS AND SENSITIVE MATERIAL.—A reporter who obtains the data of another user or any sensitive material shall immediately, and prior to submitting a report, delete every copy of that data or sensitive material, recording only the minimum non-sensitive details, such as the endpoint, timestamp, and location, needed to report the flaw.

    (d) CRAFTED .V31NP FILES.—A reporter may use a crafted .v31np file only to test whether the reading of its header or of its files leads to an overflow or another flaw that allows an exploit.

    (e) REWARDS.—No reporter is entitled to any bounty, fee, or other reward for a test or report. Any reward, bounty, or recognition is granted solely at the fiat and discretion of v31null.

    (f) FORGERY IN AUTOMATED VERIFICATION TESTS.—Nothing in subsection (b)(11) prohibits submitting a benign forged or invalidly signed document solely to test whether an automated signature, seal, or document verification check fails to reject it, provided the test uses no harmful file, targets no account of another user, and does not result in obtaining an eID or deceiving a natural person.

    (g) UNAUTHENTICATED ENDPOINTS.—Accessing or reading a page, directory, or endpoint that lacks an authentication or access gate is not by itself a violation of this policy, subject to section 6 and section 9(c) where sensitive material is involved. However, if that endpoint or resource is reached or accessed by means of, or downstream of, an exploit or any unauthorized or prohibited act under subsection (b), that downstream access is unauthorized.

    (h) AUTOMATED TOOLS AND AUTONOMOUS AGENTS.—A reporter is strictly responsible for every request, payload, and action executed by any automated scanner, script, bot, or autonomous or artificial-intelligence agent operated or deployed by the reporter.

SEC. 6. RULE OF CONSTRUCTION; LAWFUL RECEIPT.

    (a) IN GENERAL.—Notwithstanding any other provision of this policy, a reporter does not violate section 4(b)(2) or section 4(b)(12) solely because an owned system transmits, displays, or otherwise makes available to the reporter any data or sensitive material in the course of—
        (1) normal, authorized use of an account of the reporter; or
        (2) a test otherwise permitted under this policy.

    (b) CONTINUING DUTIES.—Subsection (a) excuses only the receipt or observation of the data or sensitive material. If the material received is data of another user or sensitive material not belonging solely to an account of the reporter, the reporter shall still—
        (1) cease further access to that material;
        (2) immediately delete every copy of that material in accordance with section 4(c); and
        (3) refrain from publishing, exploiting, or using that material in accordance with section 4(b)(6).

SEC. 7. GOOD-FAITH SECURITY RESEARCH.

    (a) GOOD-FAITH REQUIREMENT.—Authorization under section 4(a) applies only to good-faith security research, meaning testing an owned system solely for the purpose of finding, confirming, or reporting a problem in good faith, carried out in a manner designed to avoid harm to individuals, other users, protected services, or the public, and used solely to inform v31null so that the problem may be remedied.

    (b) LAWFUL AND AUTHORIZED CONDUCT.—Where a reporter conducts a test strictly in good faith and in full compliance with this policy, including sections 3, 4, 5, and 6, that testing is lawful and authorized under the law and policy of v31null, and v31null shall take no enforcement action, account sanction, or proceeding against the reporter for that compliant testing.

    (c) REVOCATION AND TERMINATION OF AUTHORIZATION.—Testing is not in good faith, and any authorization or protection under this policy ceases immediately as to the non-compliant conduct, if the reporter—
        (1) violates any prohibition in section 4(b);
        (2) makes a threat or acts for extortion, coercion, financial demand, or personal gain;
        (3) fails to comply with immediate deletion under section 4(c); or
        (4) continues testing after v31null expressly revokes authorization in writing, including electronically on Prono or GitHub.

    (d) EXISTING COMPROMISES AND IN FLAGRANTE INTERVENTION.—
        (1) NON-IMMINENT COMPROMISES.—If a reporter discovers evidence of an existing third-party compromise, backdoor, or unauthorized activity on an owned system that is not causing active or imminent harm, the reporter shall not stop, alter, or interfere with the service, and shall promptly report the discovery under section 3.
        (2) EMERGENCY IN FLAGRANTE INTERVENTION.—Notwithstanding section 4(b)(1) or (3), where an ongoing attack or compromise is causing active harm or poses imminent harm to users, data, or an owned system, proportionate defensive intervention strictly necessary to halt that active or imminent harm constitutes good-faith conduct under this policy, provided the reporter—
            (A) takes only the minimum action strictly necessary to stop the active or imminent harm;
            (B) ceases immediately once the imminent harm is halted and submits an Urgent report under section 3 and section 5; and
            (C) proves the active or imminent harm, necessity, and proportionality with verifiable receipts, including logs, captures, timestamps, and technical records.
        v31null recognizes this defence solely under v31null’s own law and policy and cannot confer authorization over third-party systems that v31null does not have jurisdiction over.
    (e) COMPUTATIONAL BOUNDARIES AND SANDBOXES.—
        (1) PUBLICLY REACHABLE SYSTEMS AND BOUNDARIES.—Where a publicly reachable owned system, boundary, or sandbox separates accounts, files, roles, or areas by code, configuration, or access controls, authorization extends only to the reporter’s authorized area. The failure, weakness, or bypass of a technical barrier does not authorize crossing it: a reporter may confirm with minimal, non-destructive proof that the barrier fails, but shall stop at the threshold without enumerating, browsing, altering, or pivoting into the restricted area, another system, data of another user, or sensitive material.
        (2) SANDBOXES EXPLICITLY CARVED FOR A REPORTER.—Where an active sandbox or scoped environment is explicitly carved for a reporter under section 8(d)(2), testing is authorized solely within the exact boundaries and scope carved out in that instrument. A reporter may not attempt to escape, exceed, or pivot out of that sandbox—nor rely on boundary-testing under paragraph (1) as a defence—unless that sandbox is itself publicly reachable or testing that isolation boundary is expressly authorized in the section 8(d)(2) instrument.

SEC. 8. EXCLUSIVITY OF WRITTEN POLICY; LIMITATIONS ON AGENCY AND INSTRUCTIONS.

    (a) IN GENERAL.—Only the express text of this policy grants authorization under section 4(a) or section 7. No oral statement, voice communication, informal message, implied consent, custom, or course of dealing may grant authorization, waive a prohibition under section 4(b), or excuse non-compliance with any requirement of this policy, including section 5(c).

    (b) NO AUTHORITY IN REPRESENTATIVES OR OPERATORS.—No attorney, legal representative, agent, proxy, holder of a power of attorney, Help Desk operator, registration officer, administrator, or third party has authority to permit a test, authorize access to data or sensitive material, suspend any safeguard, or waive any provision of this policy, whether acting or purporting to act on behalf of v31null or on behalf of any user.

    (c) INSTRUCTIONS AND SUPERIOR ORDERS NO DEFENCE.—It shall not be a defence or excuse, for any reporter, operator, administrator, representative, or other person, that an act prohibited by this policy was ordered, requested, directed, approved, or tolerated, orally or in writing, by any superior, attorney, operator, administrator, or person purporting to act for v31null. Each person remains individually bound by this policy and individually responsible for compliance with it.

    (d) SOLE MANNER OF AMENDMENT OR EXCEPTION.—An amendment to, or exception from, this policy is effective only if it is—
        (1) published by v31null as an amendment to this policy; or
        (2) set out in an express written instrument, subject to all of the following conditions:
            (A) OFFICIAL PADES SIGNATURE AND STATUS.—The instrument must bear a valid, intact electronic signature personally created by v31null using a v31null eID or v31null TL-signing certificate, verified by the relying person against the v31null trusted list, certificate revocation lists, and OCSP responder as valid and unrevoked at the time of reliance.
            (B) ACTUAL EXISTENCE OF TARGET.—The specific sandbox, environment, endpoint, or scope designated in the instrument must actually exist and be currently provisioned and active at the time of the act.
            (C) INACTIVE AUTHORIZATION.—If the target or sandbox designated in the instrument does not exist, has not yet been provisioned, or has been decommissioned or withdrawn, the authorization is an inactive authorization, even though the signed instrument remains unrevoked, and confers no permission to probe for, infer, or test any system.
    No oral assurance, unsigned writing, or signature by proxy satisfies this subsection.

SEC. 9. CLASSIFICATION OF DEVIATIONS: VERT, OR, AND GULES.

    (a) VERT.—A deviation falls within Vert if it involves neither data of another user nor sensitive material and causes no disruption or degradation to any protected service, such as an empty ping, negligible resource overhead, or the exposure of cold, non-secret application code containing no credentials, environment files, keys, or user data. Conduct falling strictly within Vert is exempt from sanction or violation under this policy regardless of the person’s broader intent; any conduct outside Vert remains governed by its own tier.

    (b) OR.—A deviation falls within Or if it involves data of another user, without involving sensitive material and without reckless or intentional disruption of a protected service, and the reporter establishes a provable absence of criminal intent, even where positive good intent is not shown. Any disposition or waiver for conduct within Or rests solely in the discretion of v31null on a case-by-case basis, considering immediate cessation, immediate deletion under section 4(c), and prompt reporting.

    (c) GULES.—A deviation falls within Gules if it involves any unauthorized access to, probing for, retention of, failure to immediately delete, or disclosure of sensitive material, including environment files, credentials, cryptographic keys, FRNIs, Help Desk identity documents, private eID keys, and non-public internal records, save solely for passive receipt under section 6(a) immediately cured under section 6(b) and section 4(c). Gules is subject to strict zero tolerance, with no exception, no discretionary mitigation under subsection (b), and complete forfeiture of any protection under section 7.

    (a) IN GENERAL.—This policy applies to each Prinzian system, in addition to and without replacing any other jurisdiction or law.

    (b) MEF SYSTEMS.—The positive powers, the negative powers, and the non-interesting powers apply in full to each MEF system.

    (c) OTHER PRINZIAN SYSTEMS.—Only the legal minimum applies to a Prinzian system that is not an MEF system, including that of a non-governmental organisation or a private individual. For such a system—
        (1) the non-interesting powers are subject to the security declaration policies of its owner; and
        (2) in the negative powers, “owned system” is read as that system and “v31null” as its owner.

    (d) BINDING EFFECT.—This policy binds by itself, without adoption, each agency under MEF and each organisation registered to Prinz, which shall honour it.

    (e) INTERVENTION BY MEF.—
        (1) GROUNDS.—MEF may intervene retrospectively, but is not bound to, where—
            (A) an agency or organisation fails to comply with this policy;
            (B) it is unclear whether a system falls under this policy; or
            (C) the security declaration policy of an owner lacks a provision.
        Non-conformance with a standard is not by itself grounds for intervention.
        (2) FORM.—Each finding, order, and measure shall be a PDF signed by MEF with an eID and shall be made public on the official platforms of MEF. No oral or informal finding, order, or measure has effect.
        (3) CURE PERIOD.—MEF may take a measure beyond an order to comply only if the owner has not corrected within 14 days of the order to comply.
        (4) ANSWER AND RECONSIDERATION.—The owner may answer a finding within the cure period and may ask MEF to reconsider any finding, order, or measure.
        (5) PROPORTIONALITY.—MEF shall take the mildest measure under section 2(10B) that suffices.
        (6) SCOPE.—A measure reaches only the system and the conduct named in the finding. A Practicer General sent to a system of a non-governmental organisation or a private individual sees only what the finding concerns.
        (7) DURATION.—A network ban or suspension lifts once the owner corrects and otherwise lapses at the end of the term set in it, unless MEF renews it.
        (8) RELEVANCE.—MEF may intervene, including after a system is discontinued or its owner is dissolved, only while the failure or gap remains relevant at the time of the intervention.
        (9) STANDARD.—Conduct is judged by the more lenient of the policy in force at the time of the conduct and the current policy.
        (10) EMERGENCY.—For a matter falling within Gules under section 9(c), or of equivalent gravity, MEF may take any measure under section 2(10B), out of order and without the cure period under paragraph (3), for 24 hours only. If the owner does not comply within those 24 hours, MEF may impose a network ban unilaterally.
